# Abstract

Primitive shape recognition from touch is difficult because each tactile image captures only a local contact patch rather than the complete object. This paper presents a pose-free visuo-tactile pipeline that learns interpretable local geometric features for primitive-shape recognition. GelSight Mini contact images are collected from primitive objects and labeled using seven local feature classes: planar contact, single curvature, double curvature, straight edge, circular rim, multi-face vertex, and sharp apex. A ResNet-18 classifier is trained to predict these features from raw tactile images, and the predicted features are accumulated by a rule-based shape-identification stage. The local feature classifier achieved 99.60% accuracy on a held-out test set, with errors limited to circular-rim contacts that were occasionally confused with planar contact or straight edge. Across five 30-touch trials for each primitive shape, the rule-based stage produced correct final shape predictions under both in-order and random feature presentations; cone reached at least 90% confidence within 4-7 touches, while cylinder remained the most conservative case at approximately 89% final confidence. These results show that local geometric features provide a compact and interpretable intermediate representation between raw visuo-tactile contacts and object-level primitive-shape recognition.
