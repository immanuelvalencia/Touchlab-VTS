# Pose-Free Local Geometric Feature Learning for Visuo-Tactile Primitive Shape Recognition

Robotic shape perception is difficult when visual observations are incomplete, occluded, or ambiguous during contact. Tactile sensing addresses this limitation by measuring physical interaction directly, but the information it provides is inherently local. Vision-based tactile sensors are especially useful in this setting because they convert contact-induced elastomer deformation into high-resolution image measurements of surface geometry (Yuan et al., 2017). Compact optical sensors such as DIGIT further make tactile imaging practical for manipulation research by reducing cost, size, and fabrication barriers (Lambeta et al., 2020). These advances make touch a rich geometric signal, but they do not remove the central inference problem: a single contact observes only a small part of an object.

Visuo-tactile reconstruction studies show how local tactile measurements can complement global visual observations. Vision supplies coarse object context, while touch contributes high-resolution evidence at the contact site (Smith et al., 2020). ShapeMap 3-D uses GelSight measurements with depth sensing to incrementally refine object shape through spatial mapping (Suresh et al., 2022). TouchSDF predicts local surface meshes from tactile images before reconstructing object shape with an implicit signed-distance representation (Comi et al., 2023), and VTacO uses visual-tactile sensing to improve in-hand reconstruction under rigid and deformable object interactions (Xu et al., 2023). These methods establish that tactile images encode useful local geometry, yet they mainly target reconstruction and often rely on registered contact information, spatial integration, or robot-mediated sensing.

Recent representation-learning and handheld demonstration systems broaden the role of touch beyond reconstruction. Self-supervised tactile pretraining can learn transferable tactile features without task-specific labels (Higuera et al., 2024), and unified 3D visuo-tactile representations can improve fine-grained manipulation policies by preserving spatial relationships between visual and tactile signals (Huang et al., 2025). Handheld interfaces also reduce dependence on fixed robot setups by enabling portable demonstration collection (Chi et al., 2024), while visuo-tactile grippers add synchronized contact sensing to in-the-wild manipulation data (Zhu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026). However, these systems primarily optimize policy learning, demonstration transfer, or broad representation transfer. Less attention is given to the intermediate perception problem of assigning interpretable local geometric meaning to individual, pose-free tactile contacts.

This paper studies pose-free local geometric feature learning for visuo-tactile primitive shape recognition. We define a local feature vocabulary for primitive-shape perception, collect handheld GelSight contact images without requiring known contact pose or registered surface location, train a CNN encoder to classify each contact into geometric feature labels, and evaluate the representation through feature-level accuracy and confusion analysis. The aim is not to solve full multi-touch shape recognition in this paper, but to isolate the local perception layer that makes later evidence accumulation interpretable. By converting raw tactile images into explicit local geometric predictions, the proposed pipeline provides a compact bridge between handheld visuo-tactile sensing and downstream primitive-shape inference.
